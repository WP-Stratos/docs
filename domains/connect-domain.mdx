Connecting a custom domain gives your WordPress site a real, branded web address instead of a placeholder URL. Whether you use WP Stratos's built-in DNS management or keep DNS with your existing registrar, this page walks through every step, from adding the domain in the Cloud Panel to confirming SSL is live.

<Note>
  Before you begin, make sure you have a domain name registered with any domain registrar (such as GoDaddy, Namecheap, or Cloudflare Registrar) and at least one WP Stratos site already created in your Cloud Panel.
</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Log in to the Cloud Panel">
    Go to [cloud.wpstratos.com](https://cloud.wpstratos.com) and sign in with your WP Stratos credentials.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open Change Domain">
    From your dashboard, open the site you want to connect a domain to, then click **Change Domain** under your instance's name. This opens the **Set Your Domain** window.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your domain and choose how to map it">
    Type in your domain (for example, `yourdomain.com`) and click **Change** if you need to correct it. Below that, you'll see two  sections: **Set DNS Records** and **Set Nameservers**. Pick whichever matches how you want to manage this domain going forward.

    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="Set DNS Records">
        Keep DNS management wherever it already lives (your registrar, Cloudflare, anywhere) and just point this one domain at WP Stratos. No nameserver change required.

        1. Log in to your domain provider's panel.
        2. Set these DNS records exactly as shown in your Cloud Panel modal, using your domain in place of `yourdomain.com`:

        | Name | Type | Address |
        | --- | --- | --- |
        | `yourdomain.com` (or `@`) | A | _(shown in the Cloud Panel)_ |
        | `yourdomain.com` (or `@`) | A | _(shown in the Cloud Panel)_ |
        | `www.yourdomain.com` | CNAME | `yourdomain.com` |

        That's two A records on the root domain and one CNAME for `www` pointing back at the root. The exact IP addresses are generated for your specific domain and shown right there in the modal, so copy them directly rather than reusing values from an old setup or another site.

        3. Wait anywhere from 2 minutes to a few hours for the records to propagate.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="Set Nameservers">
        Hand DNS authority for this domain over to WP Stratos's managed Cloudflare DNS. You'll get a DNS zone editor inside the Cloud Panel, plus the option to enable the Cloudflare proxy on individual records later if you ever need it.

        1. Log in to your domain provider's panel.
        2. Remove your domain's current nameservers (whatever they happen to be, your registrar's default, a previous host, and so on).
        3. Set the nameservers to the pair shown in your Cloud Panel modal. These are generated per domain, something like `norm.ns.cloudflare.com` and `thea.ns.cloudflare.com`, and will be different for every domain you connect. Always copy the exact values shown to you rather than reusing a pair from a different site.
        4. Wait anywhere from 2 minutes up to a few hours for the nameservers to propagate.

        <Warning>
          Switching nameservers does **not** migrate your existing DNS records over automatically. Anything you have set up at your old provider (MX records for email, TXT records for SPF/DKIM, CNAME records for third-party services) needs to be recreated inside the WP Stratos Cloud Panel once the switch takes effect. Write these down before you make the change. See [DNS Configuration](/domains/dns-configuration) for how to add them back.
        </Warning>
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for DNS propagation">
    After updating records or nameservers, allow time for the change to spread across the internet. Most of the time this takes somewhere between 2 minutes and a few hours, though it can occasionally stretch to 48 hours depending on your previous TTL settings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm SSL is active">
    Once your domain resolves correctly, WP Stratos automatically provisions a free Let's Encrypt SSL certificate for your site. In the Cloud Panel, check the domain's SSL status, a green indicator confirms your certificate is active and HTTPS is enabled.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  DNS propagation time varies depending on your previous TTL settings and your visitors' DNS resolvers. You can use a tool like [whatsmydns.net](https://www.whatsmydns.net) to check propagation status from multiple locations worldwide.
</Info>

## Registrar-Specific Instructions

Where you click "DNS" or "Nameservers," and what it's called once you get there, changes by registrar. Here's a map for the six you'll run into most.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="GoDaddy">
    Sign in and open **My Products**. Click the domain, then the three-dot menu next to it, then **Manage DNS**.

    For DNS records, add the two A records and the `www` CNAME under the **Records** section. GoDaddy shows your existing records first, so delete any old A record pointing at `@` before adding the new ones. Otherwise both resolve and only one wins.

    For nameservers, scroll to **Nameservers**, click **Change**, select **Enter my own nameservers (advanced)**, and paste in the pair from your Cloud Panel.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Namecheap">
    Go to **Domain List**, find the domain, and click **Manage**.

    For DNS records, open the **Advanced DNS** tab and use **Add New Record** for each row. Namecheap defaults new domains to "Namecheap BasicDNS," which is fine, you're just adding records on top of it.

    For nameservers, stay on the main **Domain** tab, find **Nameservers**, switch the dropdown to **Custom DNS**, and enter both values.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Cloudflare Registrar">
    Open the domain in your Cloudflare dashboard and click the **DNS** tab.

    Add the A and CNAME records there. Cloudflare Registrar domains already run on Cloudflare nameservers, so there's no separate nameserver step, you're managing DNS directly. One thing worth getting right: leave the proxy status (the orange cloud icon) off for these records and keep it set to **DNS only**. WP Stratos runs its own Edge Cache in front of your site, and stacking Cloudflare's proxy on top of that causes cache conflicts rather than extra protection. See [DNS Configuration](/domains/dns-configuration) for the full explanation.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Squarespace Domains (formerly Google Domains)">
    Log in, go to **Domains**, select the domain, and open the **DNS Settings** tab.

    For DNS records, scroll to **Custom Records** and add each row there.

    For nameservers, look for **Nameservers** on the same page, choose **Use custom nameservers**, and replace whatever's listed with the pair from your Cloud Panel.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Bluehost">
    From the Bluehost dashboard, go to **Domains**, select the domain, and open the **DNS** tab, sometimes labeled **Zone Editor**.

    Records and nameservers live on the same screen here. Add the A and CNAME records under **Zone Editor**, or scroll down to **Name Servers** if you're switching those instead.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Hostinger">
    Go to **Domains**, click the domain, and open **DNS / Nameservers** from the left menu.

    The DNS records table and the nameserver switch both sit on this page, just under different tabs. Add rows under **DNS Records**, or flip to **Nameservers** and choose **Change nameservers** if that's the route you're taking.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Not seeing your registrar here? The pattern holds everywhere: find DNS or Nameservers in your domain's settings, then use the values from the **Set Your Domain** window above. For a slower, more detailed walkthrough of the nameserver path specifically, see [Nameserver Setup](/domains/nameserver-setup).

## Setting or Changing Your Main Domain

If you've added more than one domain to a site, one of them is designated the **main domain**, marked with a filled star icon in your domain list. Any addon domain can be promoted to main: click the empty star next to it, confirm the change, and WP Stratos updates the hosting account to reflect the new main domain automatically. This is worth knowing if you rebrand a client site down the road and need the new domain to take over as primary without rebuilding the connection from scratch.

<Tip>
  If you're managing DNS through WP Stratos and see a proxy toggle next to a record, leave it off unless you have a specific reason to enable it. WP Stratos Edge Cache already handles caching and traffic protection at the platform level, and running both at once tends to cause more caching problems than it solves. See [DNS Configuration](/domains/dns-configuration) for the full explanation.
</Tip>
